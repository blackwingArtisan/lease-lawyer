# Product: Lease Lawyer

## One line
Ask your lease a plain question and get an answer you can check: a Prolog engine proves it step by step, and every step cites a lease clause, a rule of the model code, or something you said.

## Who it is for
Renters reading a lease they did not write, and the tenant-union volunteers and housing counselors who help them. They want to know "can my landlord do this?" and, just as much, *why*.

## The promise
- **No answer without a proof.** Yes and No are shown only when the rule engine proves them from known facts.
- **Every step cites its source.** A lease step quotes the exact clause text and links to it. A law step names the model-code section. A tenant step says "from your question" or "from your answer".
- **Unknown says what is missing.** When neither answer is provable, the app names the fact it needs and offers it as a Yes/No question, then finishes the proof.
- **Nothing invented.** A fact read from the lease (by rules or by an AI model) is kept only if its quote appears verbatim in the clause it cites.
- **Not legal advice.** The rule base is an invented "model jurisdiction". The footer says so on every page.

## The Frankenstein stitch
1970s logic programming (Prolog, a proof-producing meta-interpreter, closed-world reasoning, abduction for "what would it take") stitched to a modern language model that turns clauses into facts. The model reads; Prolog decides.

## Tone and look
A haunted law library after midnight: leather spines, parchment, brass. The proof chain lights up like electrodes in a lab, one step at a time. Spooky, never gory. Plain English in every answer.

## Out of scope
Real jurisdictions, tenant accounts, saving leases on a server, legal advice.
